Front bench setup

Students bring their board to the front and hook it up to the bench function generator, oscilloscope and power supply. Watch them make these connections: it is part of the 10 equipment marks.

  1. Function generator → signal to A, ground to F. Square wave, 2 Hz, 10 Vpp (±5 V), 0 V offset. Confirm on the scope that A really is ±5 V; a generator set for a 50 Ω load can show double the amplitude.
  2. Power supply → 9 V. The + terminal goes to circuit ground and the − terminal goes to the top rail (R4/R7). This makes the −9 V negative supply the PNP stage needs. Set a current limit of about 100 mA if the supply has one.
  3. Oscilloscope → both probe ground clips on circuit ground (F) only. DC coupling on both channels (AC coupling hides the clamp shift). About 5 V/div and 100–200 ms/div, trigger on CH1.
  4. One common ground for generator, supply and scope. Switch on the supply first, then enable the generator output.

Solder joints

  • Good: shiny, smooth, concave cone; lead outline visible; pad fully wetted; lead trimmed short.
  • Excess solder: blobs or balls that hide the lead.
  • Short: a bridge between neighbouring pads. Check the three transistor legs closely.
  • Dry joint: dull, grainy or cracked; solder sitting on the lead without wetting the pad.

Expected: LED behaviour

Green LED1 and red LED2 flash alternately, each about twice a second (2 Hz input, 500 ms period, about 250 ms on each). They should never be on together.

  • Input A in its negative half → B ≈ −9 V → Q1 on → green on; H ≈ 0 V so Q2 is off.
  • Input A in its positive half → B ≈ +0.7 V → Q1 off → H goes negative → Q2 on → red on.

Expected: waveforms

Scope on DC coupling, 5 V/div, 100–200 ms/div. Reference captures are from the Marking Guide; click to enlarge.

A and B

  • A: 10 Vpp square wave, ±5 V, period 500 ms (2 Hz).
  • B: same shape and size, shifted down: top ≈ +0.7 V, bottom ≈ −9 V (guide Vmin −9.2 V).
  • This is the negative clamper at work.

C and D

  • C: identical to B (same node).
  • D: same 10 V swing shifted further down, bottom about −17.6 V.

B and H

  • H (Q1 collector) switches between ≈ 0 V and ≈ −7 to −9 V (guide Vmin −8.8 V).
  • H is inverted relative to B: H sits at 0 V while B is low.

H and I

  • H and I are opposite square waves (180° apart).
  • I (Q2 collector) switches between ≈ 0 V and ≈ −6.6 V, in step with B.

Expected: Multisim

Demonstrated on the students’ own laptop. Accept the Figure 1 circuit or their Q7 modification (diodes reversed for a positive shift, NPN transistors, +9 V supply, LEDs reversed).

  • 8 marks: Simulate ▸ Run (F5). The two LED indicators blink alternately.
  • 6 marks: Tektronix virtual scope(s) correctly connected and showing A & B, and H & I.

Viva question bank

Fundamental questions with model answers. Each viva opens with one of your model questions, then adds questions drawn at random from the other topics. Questions are not repeated within a group, and the draw favours questions earlier groups have not heard.

Mark scheme

The circuit is graded out of 60, using the criteria on the Marking Guide’s “Total marks: 60” page. Every criterion, including the viva, is a group mark, so both students receive the same total.

One adjustment. The Marking Guide gives “Award 3 marks” for each soldering-quality item, which adds to 6, but the section is worth 4. This tool uses 2 + 2 so the total is exactly 60.
